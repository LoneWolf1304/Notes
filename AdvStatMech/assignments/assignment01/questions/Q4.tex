\begin{question}[Antiferromagnetic Ising Model]
Consider the Ising model on a two-dimensional square lattice with nearest neighbour interaction but with the interaction term having opposite sign such that antiparallel allignment is favoured.
$$\SH = J\sum\limits_{\avg{ij}} \sigma_i \sigma_j -h\sum\limits_i \sigma_i\qquad J>0$$
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] Show that for $h = 0$, the partition function and hence the free energy is the same as that for the usual Ising model.
    \item[(b)]  Solve the problem in the mean field approximation with $h = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{question}
\noindent
\textit{Answer.} (a) Let us divide the 2D lattice into two sub-lattices with sites $A$ and site $B$, alternately placed. Hence, there are only sites of type $B$ surrounding any site of type $A$ (and vice versa). Let us consider the zero-field Ising model on this sublattice. 
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering 
    \input{sublattice.tex}
    \caption{2D lattice broken into interpenetrating sub-lattices.}
\end{figure}
\noindent 
Since interaction of spins on type A site  is only with those at type B site, the Ising Hamiltonian $\SH(h=0, J, \{\sigma_i\}) = -J\sum\limits_{\avg{ij}}\sigma_i \sigma_j$ can be written as,
\begin{equation}
    \SH(h=0, J,\{\sigma_i^A\},\{\sigma_i^B\} ) = -J\sum\limits_{\avg{ij}}\sigma_i^A \sigma_j^B
\end{equation}
From this, the obvious symmetry exhibited by the Hamiltonian is,
\begin{equation}\label{sublat}
    \SH(h=0, -J,\{\sigma_i^A\},\{\sigma_i^B\} ) = \SH(h=0, J,\{-\sigma_i^A\},\{\sigma_i^B\} ) = \SH(h=0, J,\{\sigma_i^A\},\{-\sigma_i^B\} )
\end{equation}
The zero-field partition function becomes, 
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        \SZ(h=0, -J, T) &= \sum\limits_{\{\sigma^A\}}\sum\limits_{\{\sigma^B\}} e^{-\beta \SH(0, -J,\{\sigma^A\},\{\sigma^B\} )}\\
        &=\sum\limits_{\{\sigma^A\}}\sum\limits_{\{\sigma^B\}} e^{-\beta \SH(0, J,\{ -\sigma^A\},\{\sigma^B\} )}\\
        &=\sum\limits_{\{\sigma^A\}}\sum\limits_{\{\sigma^B\}} e^{-\beta \SH(0, J,\{\sigma^A\},\{\sigma^B\} )}\\
        &=\SZ(h=0, J, T)
    \end{split}
\end{align}
Going from the first to the second line, we used the sub-lattice symmetry from Eq. \eqref{sublat} while going from the second to the third line, we used the fact the since we are summing over all values of $\sigma_i = \pm$, the sum with $\{ -\sigma\}$ will be the same as the sum with $\{ +\sigma\}$. Since the free energy is just the derivative of the partition function, we obtain 
\begin{equation}
    F(h=0, J, T) = F(h=0, -J, T)
\end{equation}
Hence the partition function and the free energy for the ferromagnetic ($J>0$) and the anti-ferromagnetic ($J<0$) Ising model is same and hence, both these are thermodynamically equivalent.\\[0.3cm]
(b) We apply the mean-field approximation where we neglect the fluctuations in the spin.
\begin{equation}
    (m\tund{A} - \sigma^A_i) (m\tund{B} - \sigma^B_j) = m\tund{A}m\tund{B} -m\tund{A}\sigma_j^B - m\tund{B}\sigma_i^A + \sigma_i^A \sigma_j^B = 0
\end{equation}
Substituting this in the anti-ferromagnetic Hamiltonian with $J>0$ we get,
\begin{align*}
    \SH = J \sum\limits_{\avg{ij}} \sigma_i^A \sigma_j^B &= Jm\tund{A} \sum\limits_{\avg{ij}}\sigma_j^B + Jm\tund{B} \sum\limits_{\avg{ij}}\sigma_j^A -J m\tund{A}m\tund{B}\sum\limits_{\avg{ij}} 1 -h \sum_{i}\sigma_i^A -h\sum_{j}\sigma_j^B\\
    &=-(h-Jm\tund{A}\gamma)\sum\limits_i \sigma_i^B -(h-Jm\tund{B}\gamma)\sum\limits_i \sigma_i^A - \frac{Jm\tund{A}m\tund{B}\gamma}{2}\\
    &= -h\tund{A}\sum\limits_i \sigma_i^B -h\tund{B}\sum\limits_i \sigma_i^A -  \frac{Jm\tund{A}m\tund{B}\gamma}{2}
\end{align*}
The partition function becomes, 
$$\SZ = \SZ\tund{A}^{N/2} \SZ\tund{B}^{N/2} e^{-\beta J \gamma m\tund{A}m\tund{B}/2 }$$
where $\SZ\tund{A} = 2\cosh(\beta h\tund{B})$ and $\SZ\tund{B} = 2\cosh(\beta h\tund{A})$. The sublattice magnetisation can be obtained from the partition function as,
\begin{equation}
    m\tund{A} = \tanh(\beta h\tund{B}) \qquad m\tund{B} = \tanh(\beta h\tund{A})
\end{equation}
which are two coupled transcendental equations. In the zero field, $h\tund{A} = -Jm\tund{A}\gamma$ and $h\tund{B} = -Jm\tund{B}\gamma$. Expanding $\tanh(x)$ for $x\ll 1$ we get, 
\begin{align}\label{magg}
    \begin{split}
         m\tund{A} &= - \tanh(m\tund{B} \frac{T\tund{c}}{T}) \simeq -\frac{T\tund{c}}{T}m\tund{B}  +\frac{1}{3}\qty(\frac{T\tund{c}}{T})^3 m\tund{B}^3\\
     m\tund{B} &= - \tanh(m\tund{A} \frac{T\tund{c}}{T}) \simeq -\frac{T\tund{c}}{T}m\tund{A}  +\frac{1}{3}\qty(\frac{T\tund{c}}{T})^3 m\tund{A}^3
    \end{split}
\end{align}
Define $M := \frac{m\tund{A}+m\tund{B}}{2}$ and $m\tund{s} := \frac{m\tund{A}-m\tund{B}}{2}$ which allows us to write,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{split}
        m\tund{A}^3+m\tund{B}^3 = 2M(M^2 +3m\tund{s}^2)\\
         m\tund{A}^3-m\tund{B}^3 = 2m\tund{s}(3M^2 +m\tund{s}^2)
    \end{split}
\end{equation}
From Eq. \eqref{magg}, by adding and subtracting we have: 
\begin{align}
    \begin{split}
        M\qty(1+\frac{T\tund{c}}{T}) = \frac{M}{3}\qty(\frac{T\tund{c}}{T})^3\qty[M^2+3m\tund{s}^2]\\
        m\tund{s}\qty(1-\frac{T\tund{c}}{T}) = -\frac{m\tund{s}}{3}\qty(\frac{T\tund{c}}{T})^3\qty[3M^2+m\tund{s}^2]
    \end{split}
\end{align}
The solution of the second equation for  is $m\tund{s} =0 $ or $h$
